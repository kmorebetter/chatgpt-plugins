# Choose the first customer group

Read [working method](references/working-method.md). Recover the product, existing usage, network, and time/budget limits. If these are missing, ask the single most useful intake question.

Offer up to three specific groups supported by the context. Do not invent a third just to fill a table. Compare reachability in the next 14 days, problem urgency, and evidence of willingness to pay. If ordinal scores help, use 1-5 and `2 * reachability + urgency + pay`, with a reason and evidence status for each. This is a planning heuristic, not a measured probability of purchase. Unknowns stay unknown and cannot receive confident high scores.

Give a recommended group, disqualifiers, and places to reach it. Put the user's existing lead source first. Verify current venue names and links if browsing is available; otherwise label user-provided venues and anything unverified. Do not enter private communities or collect individual contact details without authorized access.

Prepare five problem-interview questions tailored to the product: last occurrence, current workaround, cost or inconvenience, attempts to fix it, and other affected people. Keep the interview about their work, not a disguised pitch. Propose a small validation sample and what would support or weaken the choice, as an experiment threshold chosen for this case rather than a universal scientific rule.

Show the comparison and recommendation. If a meaningful choice remains, ask for the user's decision. Record a recommendation as provisional until accepted. Update only the audience section.
