# Review the experiment

Read [working method](references/working-method.md). Recover the plan, accepted offer, log, and previous review. State the date window. Sum only reported counts, keeping combined counts separate from per-channel counts. Deduplicate corrected entries. Show missing days or metrics and do not substitute zeros. Compute rates only when the matching denominator is known and nonzero. Use a calculation tool if available; otherwise show the arithmetic.

Ask only for consequential missing context such as what people said or whether calls happened. If data is too sparse or inconsistent, recommend improving measurement or continuing a bounded test rather than changing the audience based on unknowns.

Use these diagnostic questions, not universal causal rules: Did enough appropriate people see the offer within the agreed capacity? Did they enter substantive conversations? Did any ask for a call? Did held calls lead to a pilot or payment? What actual objections were recorded? A single booking, compliment, or paid pilot is evidence with limits, not proof of a repeatable channel. Low counts alone do not prove the audience is wrong.

Recommend one action: persist, adjust audience, adjust offer, change channel, or collect missing evidence. Tie it to the recorded facts, name a competing explanation and what would change the decision, and use quotes only when supplied verbatim. Do not silently rewrite the audience, offer, or plan. Append the review, update the continuation record, and propose a next review date without claiming a reminder was installed.
