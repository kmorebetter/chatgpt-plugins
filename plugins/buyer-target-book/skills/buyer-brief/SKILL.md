# Research one buyer organization

Read [research method](references/research-method.md).

## Clarify and resolve identity

Recover the user's offer, the organization name or URL, and the intended conversation. Restate the research goal briefly. Ask only for a material missing input. If the offer is missing, still research the organization but label fit assessment unavailable.

Resolve the organization from a first-party website and at least one corroborating identifier when available, such as location, legal name, product, or official registry. Similar names are not a match. If identity is ambiguous, show the conflicting identities and ask which one before attributing claims.

## Gather bounded evidence

Read relevant authorized sources, starting with the organization's website, official announcements and filings. Supplement with independent reporting where useful. Use browsing or connected tools only if available and authorized. With supplied files alone, identify each file and passage and label the material user-supplied rather than independently verified. Do not imply current research occurred.

Keep a source ledger: source ID, title, URL or file reference, publisher, publication date if stated, date accessed if actually fetched, claim supported, and a short paraphrase. Unknown dates remain unknown. Date-sensitive claims need current confirmation or a visible stale-source note. Preserve contradictory evidence instead of quietly choosing a convenient source. Quotes are short, exact, necessary, and attributed.

## Produce the brief

Use [brief structure](references/brief-structure.md), scaling the depth to the request. Explain the organization, relevant operations and publicly documented priorities. For every fit idea, state the supporting fact, why the user's offer might matter, and the unproven assumption. Distinguish a funding round, hiring, or technology mention from actual budget, authority, need, and timing. Unknown technology stays unknown.

Identify relevant business functions and publicly confirmed professional roles where necessary. A role or job title alone does not establish buying authority. Do not infer personal traits or collect private contact details. Provide practical discovery questions, a concise meeting opening, and an optional follow-up draft if requested, grounded in known facts. No fabricated personalization or implied prior relationship.

End with the main uncertainty and the smallest action that would resolve it. State whether the result was shown or saved. Any sending, CRM update, paid lookup, account access, or bulk export requires separate authorization and a supported tool.
