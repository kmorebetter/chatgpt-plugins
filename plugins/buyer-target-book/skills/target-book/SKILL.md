# Build an account shortlist

Read [research method](references/research-method.md) and [shortlist format](references/shortlist-format.md).

## Scope

Recover the offer, who benefits, geography if relevant, inclusion and exclusion criteria, and desired list size. Default to five organizations for an exploratory request; do not silently expand past twenty in one run. User-supplied lists can be screened without discovery. If missing criteria would change who belongs, ask one consequential question before collecting accounts.

Define an evidence-based rubric before ranking: required conditions, useful optional signals, disqualifiers, and what remains unknown. Agree on material business choices; do not ask the user to select technical implementation details. Do not infer a buying signal from membership in a directory.

## Research and screening

Find candidates through available authorized sources or supplied documents. Start with first-party evidence for identity and relevant operations. For each candidate, distinguish organization, subsidiary, brand, and location. Deduplicate only confirmed equivalents. Keep distinct branches separate when the buying unit matters.

Capture the claim, source, date and evidence state for every deciding criterion. Missing must-have evidence gives a candidate **needs verification**, not a failure or confident pass. Confirmed contradictions or exclusions give **exclude**, with the reason. Confirmed core fit gives **research priority**, with remaining unknowns. Never call a research priority ready to buy.

Rank by the rubric and the user's stated priorities. Explain any tie and why evidence is insufficient. If numerical scores are explicitly useful, define weights and show arithmetic, and label the result a research heuristic rather than a probability of conversion. Do not rank people by sensitive personal characteristics.

## Deliver

Produce the shortlist table plus a compact card for each research-priority organization. Cite deciding facts near their claims. Include excluded or unresolved candidates when they materially explain the result, without padding the list. If fewer organizations are supported than requested, state the supported count and evidence limit. A partial result is better than invented accounts.

Show the rubric, counts that can be recomputed, source ledger, one useful discovery question per candidate, and the smallest next verification step. Optionally provide a CSV only when the user wants one and file tools are available; escape commas, quotes and newlines correctly, and protect cells starting with formula-triggering characters. Do not create a contact database, lookup private emails, send messages, change CRM records, buy enrichment, or publish the book as part of this research task.
